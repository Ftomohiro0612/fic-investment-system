## Internal Link Rules

Internal links should guide the reader to the next most useful article.

## Hard Gate Before Adding Any Related Link
- Add a related company/article link only when the connection can be explained in one sentence using a concrete driver: same customer, same supply chain, direct competitor, substitute technology, shared upstream variable, shared regulation/policy, or direct financing/capital-market exposure.
- Do not add links merely because the company is famous, in the same broad sector, recently published, likely to have an article, or SEO-friendly.
- If the link target does not answer the reader's likely next question from the current paragraph, do not include it.
- If the URL was not provided by the input pack, spreadsheet, WordPress search result, or verified existing FIC article list, do not guess the URL.
- When in doubt, omit the related link and leave the candidate in an internal memo instead of publishing it.

## Link Types To Prioritize
- Supporting explainer article
- Related company analysis
- Sector or theme overview
- Follow-up article for deeper analysis

## Placement Guidance
- Up to 2 links near the top if they improve understanding
- Natural links inside the body where a concept needs support
- A short related reading section near the end

## Anchor Text Guidance
- Keep anchor text descriptive
- Avoid repeating the same anchor text every time
- Match the reader's likely next question

## Examples Of Links To Reject
- A bank article should not link to a semiconductor company merely because both are large Japanese stocks. It is valid only when the paragraph discusses that bank's financing, underwriting, credit exposure, or capital-market transaction connected to that company or industry.
- A semiconductor memory article should not link to a bank merely because market conditions or rates are mentioned. It is valid only when financing conditions, debt refinancing, IPO, M&A funding, or direct lender exposure is part of the investment thesis.
