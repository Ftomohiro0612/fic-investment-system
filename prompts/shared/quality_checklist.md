## Quality Checklist

- Does the introduction state the conclusion early?
- Is the article aligned with the target query?
- Is there a useful summary block near the top?
- Is the first layer understandable for non-expert readers?
- Does the deeper layer add genuine analytical value?
- Are internal links helpful and intentional?
- For every related company/article, can the writer explain the concrete connection in one sentence using a driver such as same customer, supply chain, direct competitor, substitute technology, shared regulation/policy, shared upstream variable, or direct financing/capital-market exposure?
- Are any related links included only because the target is a large company, same broad sector, recently published, or SEO-friendly? If yes, remove them.
- Are risks and watch points clearly stated?
- Does the title match what the reader most wants to know, such as related companies, confirmation points, or earnings drivers?
- Are direct beneficiaries, confirmation candidates, surrounding evidence, and rejected candidates clearly separated?
- Are any statements stronger than the underlying source, such as "confirmed", "started", "transformed", or "direct benefit" without official support?
- Are surrounding events or similar cases presented as supporting context rather than as direct results of the main event?
- Does the title include at least two of: main related company name, affected industry name, reader-facing judgment axis (e.g. confirmation points, leading indicators)?
- Does the 30-second summary "caveat" item include at least one of: share-price discount level, legal binding status of the trigger event, or earnings reflection lag with a concrete timeframe?
- Are revenue and profit decomposition formulas for the lead companies exposed as a body paragraph (e.g. wrapped in a strong tag), not buried inside a table cell?
- Does the article include a one-shot vs. recurring benefit time-axis explainer (e.g. equipment vs. materials, order vs. revenue recognition vs. MRO) in a beginner-box, so readers do not misread the theme as uniform upside?
- When analyst or expert commentary is quoted, are the publishing organisation, the speaker's affiliation, and the publication month all stated in the body?
- Is the influence-path stage count (4 to 6) matched to the number of decision-maker layers, and does the body explicitly mark which stage the current event sits on?
- If a comparison article exists, does the review treat the Codex/Claude article as the base and import only reusable strengths from the comparison, such as a clearer causal path, useful keywords, or a reader-friendly framing?
- Does the article avoid turning a supply-chain concern into a supply-stoppage article when official sources describe supply continuity, policy support, inventories, substitutes, or phased recovery?
- Are cost pressure themes separated into demand-side impact, supplier-side price pass-through, and upstream spread/volume exposure instead of forcing every company into a simple beneficiary/adversary bucket?
- When a survey percentage is used, does the article explain what the denominator means so readers do not mistake a response ratio, factor ratio, or cited reason share for a revenue/cost composition ratio?
- Does the article include a classification table for related companies when the theme has mixed effects, such as adverse impact, price-pass-through candidate, spread-sensitive upstream player, and surrounding evidence?
